Speech API pricing [November 2026]

Scope of this page: speech APIs quote prices in four different units (per 1M characters, per 1M tokens, per minute, per hour). Below, text-to-speech (TTS) and transcription (STT) are converted onto a common per-minute and per-1,000-character basis. Every assumption is stated, and where a vendor publishes its own second/minute conversion we quote it verbatim. We do not rank quality or latency: vendors' claims are not comparable and we have no first-hand measurements.

●Last updated: November 15, 2026 | Official vendor pages fetched on 2026-11-15. Anything we could not verify is marked "not verified".

The three findings that change the decision

So which is cheapest? (assuming 300 characters per minute)

  • Speech generation (TTS): MAI-Voice-2.1-Flash at $0.0045/min is the cheapest (character-billed). That price comes from Microsoft’s official blog and is not yet in the verified Azure pricing table. OpenAI tts-1 is the same price but Deprecated.
  • Transcription: MAI-Transcribe-2 at $0.10/hour is the cheapest (limited-time offer through the end of the year). The regular band is Meta Muse Voice Transcribe at $0.18/hour and Gemini 3.5 Transcribe at about $0.18–0.30/hour.
  • Real-time, two-way: the Gemini 3.8 Live family at about $0.023/min (≈$1.38/hour). OpenAI gpt-realtime-2.1 publishes no per-minute audio-token conversion, so we mark it unverified.

The trap: TTS prices are published as $15 per 1M characters (OpenAI tts-1) and $9 per 1M audio tokens (Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS). $9 looks cheaper than $15. But Google states that audio tokens run at 25 tokens per second — 1,500 per minute, so 1M audio tokens is $0.0135 per minute, while $15 per 1M characters is $0.0045 per minute at 300 characters per minute. The larger-looking number is one third of the price. Comparing $9 against $15 without normalizing the unit gets the decision exactly backwards. (The OpenAI tts-1 used in this example is labelled Deprecated as of 2026-11-15 — see the table below.)

English narration is usually faster (about 900 characters per minute), so one minute of English speech consumes about three times as many characters. For character-priced models that means the per-minute cost is roughly three times the figures below, while the per-1,000-character figures do not change. For token-priced models the effect runs the other way: faster narration means fewer seconds of audio per 1,000 characters, so both columns move. Read every per-minute and per-1,000-character column on this page as a Japanese-narration baseline; only the per-1M-unit list prices are directly comparable across vendors. Rows marked † are ours: OpenAI publishes no second-to-token conversion for gpt-4o-mini-tts, so we apply Google's 25 tokens per second.

Which to pick, by job

  • Cheapest bulk transcription → MAI-Transcribe-2 ($0.10/hour, time-limited). At list prices, Meta Muse Voice Transcribe or Gemini 3.5 Transcribe, both $0.18/hour.
  • Live captions → decide on latency as well as price: MAI-Transcribe-2-Streaming (partials in ~100 ms, $0.54/hour), Gemini 3.5 Transcribe Live ($0.30–$0.54/hour) or OpenAI gpt-live-transcribe ($1.02/hour).
  • Narration and voice-overs → the cheapest current character-billed option is MAI-Voice-2.1-Flash ($0.015 per 1,000 characters); all three OpenAI character-billed TTS models are deprecated, so price new work on GPT-Live ($0.05/min, per second) or Gemini's token billing. At the top end, Gemini 3.1 Flash TTS Preview is $0.10 per 1,000 characters.
  • Voice agents → ignore the TTS rows and price both directions (about $0.023/minute on Gemini 3.8 Live). Talk time is what you pay for.
Caveat: this is a comparison of price, not of quality. Word error rates and voice quality are claimed under conditions each vendor chooses, and we have no measurements of our own under identical conditions. Before switching, run the same audio file through both.
